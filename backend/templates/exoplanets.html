What Are Exoplanets?
Exoplanets are planets that orbit stars outside our solar system. Thousands have been discovered using space telescopes and advanced detection techniques. Some may exist in habitable zones where liquid water is possible.

🛰️ Detection Methods

Transit and Radial Velocity methods allow scientists to detect exoplanets by observing starlight variations and stellar motion.

🌍 Earth-like Candidates

Examples include Kepler-452b and TRAPPIST-1e, located within their star’s habitable zone.

⭐ Stellar Influence

Star temperature and luminosity directly influence planetary climate stability.

🔭 Scientific Importance

Understanding exoplanets helps estimate the probability of life beyond Earth.

🧪 Atmospheric Analysis

Spectroscopy helps detect atmospheric gases like oxygen and methane — potential biosignatures.

📊 AI & Data Science

Machine learning models analyze astrophysical datasets to predict planetary habitability.
