Unveiling New Worlds

Exoplanets are planets beyond our solar system. Thousands have been discovered in the past two decades, revolutionizing our understanding of the cosmos.

How Do We Find Them?

Most exoplanets are found using the Transit Method. When a planet passes in front of its star, it blocks a tiny fraction of the star's light. By measuring this dip in brightness, astronomers can determine the planet's size and orbit.

Another major method is Radial Velocity (Wobble Method), where we detect the star wobbling due to the gravitational pull of an orbiting planet.

The Goldilocks Zone

The Habitable Zone (or Goldilocks Zone) is the range of distances from a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface. If it's too close, water boils away; too far, and it freezes.

Finding planets in this zone is key to the search for extraterrestrial life, though other factors like atmosphere and magnetic fields are also crucial.

Types of Exoplanets

Terrestrial

Earth-sized rocky worlds, potential homes for life.

Super-Earths

Larger than Earth, smaller than Neptune. Common in the galaxy.

Neptune-like

Gaseous worlds similar to Neptune, often with thick atmospheres.

Gas Giants

Massive planets like Jupiter, sometimes ("Hot Jupiters") orbiting very close to their stars.

Famous Discoveries

Proxima Centauri b

Our closest neighbor.

Located just 4.2 light-years away, this Earth-mass planet sits in the habitable zone of the closest star to our Sun. It is a prime target for future interstellar missions.

The TRAPPIST-1 System

7 Earth-sized worlds.

A marvel of the cosmos, this system hosts seven Earth-sized planets orbiting a red dwarf star. Three of these planets are firmly within the habitable zone.

Kepler-452b

"Earth's Cousin".

The first near-Earth-size planet found in the habitable zone of a star very similar to our Sun. It offered the first glimpse of a true "Earth 2.0".

A Brief History

1992: First Detection

Aleksander Wolszczan and Dale Frail discover two planets orbiting a pulsar (PSR B1257+12), proving planets exist outside our solar system.

1995: 51 Pegasi b

Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz discover the first planet orbiting a Sun-like star. It was a "Hot Jupiter", shocking astronomers.

2009: Kepler Mission Launch

NASA launches the Kepler Space Telescope, which would go on to discover thousands of exoplanets.

Today & Future

With James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), we are now analyzing exoplanet atmospheres for signs of water, methane, and potential biosignatures.

The Weird & Wonderful

55 Cancri e

The Diamond Planet

This super-Earth is likely covered in graphite and diamond. It's twice as wide as Earth but eight times more massive!

HD 189733 b

Raining Glass

The weather here is deadly. It rains molten glass sideways due to 5,400 mph winds!

Kepler-16b

Tatooine

A real-life Tatooine! This planet orbits two stars, meaning it has two sunsets every day.
