Prediction Guidelines
🟢 Highly Habitable (80-100)
Excellent conditions for life. Planet has favorable temperature, proper mass/radius ratio, and stable orbit. Highest potential for supporting Earth-like life.
🔵 Likely Habitable (60-79)
Good conditions with some moderate trade-offs. Temperature near habitable zone, reasonable planetary characteristics. Strong candidate for life support.
🟠 Possibly Habitable (40-59)
Marginal conditions. May support microbial life or exotic organisms. Requires favorable additional factors not captured by model.
🔴 Low Habitability (0-39)
Unfavorable conditions. Extreme temperatures, unstable orbit, or unsuitable planetary properties make life unlikely.
Temperature vs Habitability

How equilibrium temperature affects habitability predictions

The optimal temperature range for life (as we know it) is between 273.15K (0°C) and 373.15K (100°C). Planets with equilibrium temperatures outside this range are less likely to support life unless protected by atmosphere.

About Exoplanets

Exoplanets are planets outside our Solar System orbiting other stars. They vary widely in size, temperature, and composition — from small rocky worlds to gas giants much larger than Jupiter. Scientists study equilibrium temperature, stellar type, orbital period, and other factors to assess a planet's potential habitability.

Quick facts:

  • First confirmed exoplanet discovered in 1992.
  • Over 5,000 confirmed exoplanets cataloged (NASA).
  • Habitable zone depends on star brightness and planet atmosphere.
